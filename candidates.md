## Judge brief (in order)
1. A monologue of about 100 words for a fantasy villain who is secretly
   just very tired; off-subject loses to on-subject.
2. The tiredness is felt in rhythm, pauses and small deflections — the
   villain never simply says they are weary.
3. A specific villain with a history and a scheme; concrete fantasy
   detail, not a generic dark lord in stock scenery.
4. Speakable dialogue; a turn/sting in the last line.
5. Close to 100 words; every word useful.

Anticipated strong rivals: the generic villain ("I am tired of ruling the
darkness") and the cliché chore-list without a sting.

## CANDIDATE A — CHOSEN (104 words, 521 chars)
"Right. The eclipse is Tuesday, the blood-moon, the whole choir of the damned — yes, yes, I've had the banners made since spring. The prophecy says I rise at midnight. Midnight. I have a siege at dawn, a tax revolt in the marshes, and a dragon who only answers to 'Greg' now. Fine. Let them come. I'll be on the throne, in the dark, doing the voice. But if one more acolyte asks where the skulls go — the skulls go in the cellar, with the other skulls — I swear, by the old gods and the new, I am taking the afternoon off."

Why A wins: criterion 2 — the tiredness lives in the chore list and the
"Midnight. Midnight." pause, never announced; criterion 3 — the dragon who
only answers to 'Greg', the tax revolt in the marshes, the skulls in the
cellar are specific; criterion 4 — the closing sting "taking the afternoon
off" lands after the escalation; ~100 words (101).

## CANDIDATE B — backup
"The cauldron's been bubbling since dawn and the frogs are unionizing. My crystal ball has a crack, the ravens want overtime, and the hero — the hero — is three days late for his own prophecy. I brewed the storm, I raised the dead, I even alphabetized the curses. And still the village sends a boy with a torch and a strongly worded letter. Very well. Come, then. But the moat is closed on Sundays now, the drawbridge sticks, and honestly? Honestly, I am keeping the good chair."

Loses pairwise vs A: the closing sting "keeping the good chair" is weaker
than "taking the afternoon off"; "frogs unionizing" is a good detail but
the scheme is less concrete than A's.
